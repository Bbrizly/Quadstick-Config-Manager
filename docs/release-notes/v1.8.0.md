Everything below is new since 1.7.1. Most of it started as one review: Drew
Redepenning, who sets QuadSticks up for patients, went through the app in
September and wrote down every place it made him stop and think.

## The settings page says what the settings do

Joystick full deflection is called sensitivity now, because that is what it
is. Hard sip/puff threshold says normal, because that is the threshold the
ordinary sip and puff inputs use, and the hard delay says it is how long you
sip the side tube to change game file.

A group opens on the few settings people change. The joystick had thirteen
settings in one list, and the two a clinician sets every session were somewhere
in the middle of it. Each group now shows its short list and folds the rest
behind More options, and the fold opens itself when your file already carries
one of the settings inside it.

The Bluetooth dropdowns say what they do. They used to offer the firmware's own
words, so you picked between game_pad, ssp and dtr, and the pairing modes were
a bare 0, 1, 2 and 4. Every option now reads in English with the device's own
token beside it. The meanings come from the radio's command reference and from
the firmware that sends them, not from a guess.

The words on the function list are Drew's, from the sheet he sent.

## Picking an emulation mode

All eight modes are on the list. Four of them take the drive away, and those
four say so on the label instead of being hidden, because people pick them on
purpose. The one file the device boots into, `default.csv`, still refuses to
be written with one, since the way back from that is the physical force-erase.

The picker itself is one dropdown now, not nine stacked buttons, and adding a
Preferences sheet asks which controller the file should make. Nothing is
written unless you pick: a blank value is mode 0, not an empty setting.

## The picture follows what you pick

The FPS and the Original each have their own photo. Until now the FPS asset was
a copy of the classic one, so choosing your model changed nothing on screen.

Hole combos are labelled on the device, all five of them, and each is
clickable. Picking one rings the two or three holes it needs, joins them, and
fades the rest. Switch jacks and USB devices put the back of the case on the
main stage, and a Main controls row is the way back to the front.

A part's mappings read in gesture order, soft puff down to soft sip, rather
than in the order the rows sit in the file. Nothing in the file moves. The plus
that adds a mapping sits in the part heading, beside the count.

## Importing somebody else's workbook

A workbook with fifty blank template rows raised fifty warnings, and the one
warning that mattered was pushed off the bottom. Repeated warnings fold into
one line with a count. Warnings about an input nobody recognises stay one per
cell, because you answer those one at a time.

The review no longer runs off the right edge, and the output picker no longer
lets a sheet's own heading decide what you can choose: a file headed "XBox
Outputs" was hiding outputs from rows that already used them.

## Thirteen languages

Everything above is translated. The five hole pairings used to be English
built into the code, invisible while they labelled a chip and not once they
labelled the device.

## Known

Live input, and the row lighting built on it, are proved against the firmware's
own USB descriptors rather than against hardware in a room. If the dot does not
follow your stick, or a row lights for the wrong output, that is the thing to
report first.
